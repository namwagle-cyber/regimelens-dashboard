How to read this

Data sources. The current NSE 100 constituent list is from Tickertape. Each firm's D/E, ROCE, P/B, FII/DII shareholding, and 1-year price return are pulled from its Screener.in page. The regime nowcast's macro inputs are from the Press Information Bureau (GDP), MOSPI (CPI inflation), and the RBI Monetary Policy Committee (repo rate), each cited beside its figure above. Everything on this page is a point-in-time pull as of 26 Aug 2026, not a live feed.

Methodology. Cluster is a live k-means fit (k=7) run directly on these 100 NSE 100 constituents' standardized D/E, ROCE, and P/B — not a fixed rule. Each of the fit's empirical groups is relabeled to the closest-matching CL1–CL7 archetype below by comparing its centroid's Low/Moderate/High pattern on each dimension; more than one empirical group can land on the same label, and it's possible for a given fit not to produce all seven archetypes. The legend above the table shows each archetype's typical pattern alongside a cluster-mix bar with this cross-section's actual proportions.

  • CL1 — Low-Leverage Value: low debt, richly valued, middling returns on capital — conservative balance sheets the market already prices generously.
  • CL2 — Leveraged Low-Profitability: high debt and a high valuation paired with weak returns — debt or pricing not yet justified by earnings.
  • CL3 — Moderately Leveraged Average: unremarkable on all three dimensions — the broad middle of the index.
  • CL4 — High-Leverage Distressed: heavy debt and weak returns, a combination that tends to persist rather than recover.
  • CL5 — High Quality Growth: low debt, high valuation, and high returns on capital together — the strongest all-round profile in the taxonomy.
  • CL6 — Operationally Efficient: moderate debt and valuation with high returns — strong fundamentals the market hasn't fully re-rated yet.
  • CL7 — High-Growth Premium: high valuation and high returns with only moderate debt — priced for growth, a combination that has historically been hard to sustain.

1Y Return is the trailing 12-month change in each firm's share price only (not a total return — dividends aren't added back in). It's a plain price-performance figure, separate from the Cluster and FII/DII calculations, so a firm's fundamentals and its recent market performance can be read side by side.

FII / DII is each firm's latest disclosed quarterly shareholding — the % of equity held by Foreign and Domestic Institutional Investors, with an arrow showing the move versus the prior quarter. This is ownership, not day-to-day net buying/selling — it updates once a quarter, not daily, and isn't part of the Cluster calculation.

Sector shown in the table is a broad, filterable grouping derived from each firm's more specific industry classification as reported by the source.

Regime is a nowcast of which of five macroeconomic regimes today's readings most resemble. The five regimes were formed by grouping India's 2002–2024 annual GDP growth, CPI inflation, repo rate, and USD/INR movement into five clusters of years with similar macro character, each compiled into a fixed historical centroid; today's reading is matched to the nearest one by standardized (z-scored) Euclidean distance (see the note beside the ranking above). This nowcast is India-wide and not derived from the NSE 100 constituents above. What each regime represents:

  • R1 — High Growth (2002–2007): fast, broad-based expansion — GDP growth averaging ~8% with inflation and rates both contained; CL3 (Moderately Leveraged Average) was the typical firm profile of the period.
  • R2 — Slow Growth / Stagnation (2008, 2011–2014): weaker growth (~6%) alongside persistently high inflation (~9%) and high policy rates — the post-financial-crisis hangover and the early-2010s stagflation stretch.
  • R3 — Recovery / Transition (2009–2010): a short, stimulus-driven rebound — growth snapped back above 9%, but inflation ran even hotter (~11–12%), an unstable combination that didn't last.
  • R4 — Stable Moderate Growth (2015–2019, 2021–2024): steady ~7% growth with inflation mostly inside the RBI's comfort band — the most persistent regime, and the one where CL5 (High Quality Growth) firms have tended to dominate.
  • R5 — Crisis / Contraction (2020): the COVID-19 shock — GDP contracted sharply (-5.8%) even as most firms defensively re-rated toward CL1 (Low-Leverage Value).
